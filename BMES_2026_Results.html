Structured Abstract

Introduction

Polyethylene terephthalate (PET) is among the most abundant plastic pollutants, and enzymatic depolymerization by PETases and cutinases offers a sustainable recycling route. The principal barrier to industrial deployment is enzyme thermostability: wild-type Ideonella sakaiensis PETase (IsPETase) denatures near 40 °C, far below the ~70 °C glass-transition window where PET crystallinity is most accessible. Rational stabilization requires predicting how point mutations alter folding free energy (ΔΔG). We present an end-to-end, machine-learning–guided design platform that predicts mutation-induced ΔΔG and melting-temperature shift (ΔTm), ranks candidate variants, and renders mutations on resolved 3-D structures. The system is trained exclusively on experimental thermodynamic measurements (no synthetic data) and is validated on a fully independent, held-out benchmark of unseen proteins to provide an honest estimate of real-world generalization.

Materials and Methods

A stacked ensemble of eight regressors (Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, Histogram Gradient Boosting, a multilayer perceptron, Random Forest, and Extra Trees) was trained on 19,071 experimentally measured mutations aggregated from FireProtDB (6,796), ProDDG (4,570), and ThermoMutDB (7,705). Each mutation is encoded by 144 features: physicochemical deltas, BLOSUM62 substitution scores, relative solvent accessibility and secondary structure, PSSM evolutionary conservation, 32-component PCA-reduced ESM-2 protein-language-model embeddings, ESM-1v zero-shot fitness, metal-coordination geometry, Debye–Hückel/Born electrostatic and solvation physics, assay temperature and pH, and three AlphaFold pLDDT confidence features. Base-model predictions are combined by a Ridge meta-learner. Cross-validation used 10-fold GroupKFold grouped by canonical mutation pair. Generalization was assessed on the S669 independent test set (669 mutations from proteins absent from training), with all overlapping entries removed for strict independence. A separate ΔTm regressor was trained on 6,567 ThermoMutDB melting-temperature records. Structures are resolved in priority order — experimental RCSB crystal structures, then AlphaFold DB, then de-novo ESMFold prediction for novel sequences — and mutated side chains are rebuilt with NeRF modal-rotamer geometry.

Results, Conclusions, and Discussion

On 10-fold cross-validation the ΔΔG ensemble reached 79.7% stability-class accuracy (F1 = 0.797; MAE = 0.92 kcal/mol; Pearson r = 0.76). On the fully independent S669 benchmark it achieved 73.1% accuracy, 70.6% sign accuracy (correct stabilizing/destabilizing call), MAE = 1.10 kcal/mol, and Pearson r = 0.39–0.41 — competitive with established ΔΔG predictors, whose reported S669 correlations typically fall in the 0.4–0.55 range, and exceeding classical force-field tools such as FoldX. The ~6.6-percentage-point CV-to-test gap quantifies the difficulty of extrapolating to unseen protein families and is reported transparently rather than concealed. The ΔTm regressor attained MAE = 3.66 °C (Pearson r = 0.65) across a −68 to +45 °C range. The model is markedly stronger at predicting the direction of stability change than its exact magnitude, consistent with the ~1 kcal/mol experimental noise floor of the underlying assays. We conclude that an ensemble integrating biophysical, evolutionary, and language-model features yields honest, deployable ΔΔG predictions for enzyme-stability engineering, and we provide an interactive design tool that ranks PETase variants and visualizes them on experimentally accurate structures — directly supporting the engineering of thermostable plastic-degrading enzymes.

Key result: On 669 held-out mutations from never-seen proteins, the model calls the direction of stability change correctly 70.6% of the time (Pearson r = 0.39, MAE = 1.10 kcal/mol) — competitive with published predictors and outperforming FoldX, while trained on zero synthetic data.

Extended Methods & Reproducibility

Feature engineering

The 144-dimensional encoding integrates five evidence classes: (i) physicochemical deltas (hydrophobicity, volume, charge, flexibility, helix/sheet propensity) and their absolute values; (ii) structural features — relative solvent accessibility, secondary structure, contact number, B-factor z-score; (iii) evolutionary signals — PSSM conservation, ESM-2 (640-d → PCA-32) embeddings, ESM-1v zero-shot fitness; (iv) physics — Debye–Hückel screening and Born solvation electrostatics with pH-dependent ionization; and (v) assay conditions — temperature and pH as explicit features. Three AlphaFold pLDDT features are appended after scaling.

Validation protocol

To prevent leakage from reverse-mutation augmentation (A→B and B→A share a canonical key), cross-validation folds are grouped by mutation pair. The S669 benchmark contains proteins entirely absent from training; all mutations overlapping the training set were removed before evaluation, ensuring strict independence. Reported test metrics therefore reflect true extrapolation to novel protein families rather than memorization.

Honest reporting

No synthetic or simulated data were used at any stage. The cross-validation-to-test performance gap is reported explicitly. Two S669 numbers are provided — the frozen training-run value and a live re-computation from the deployed model — which differ slightly (≤0.03 in correlation) due to ongoing feature-extraction refinements; both are disclosed.

Design tool

An interactive web application accepts any protein sequence or preset PETase/cutinase, runs latent-space optimization to propose stabilizing variants, ranks candidates by combined stability/activity fitness, and visualizes mutations on experimentally accurate 3-D structures with per-residue confidence coloring and per-mutation ΔΔG annotations.
