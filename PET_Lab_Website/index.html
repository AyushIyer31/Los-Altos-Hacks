01 / The lab

We design PET-degrading enzymes for the conditions recycling actually requires.

PET Lab is a student-led research group focused on enzyme design for plastic recycling. We study how changes in protein sequence affect catalytic activity, stability, and performance under heat, pH, salt, and process-water constraints.

Our platform combines protein models with published experimental evidence. Every result is presented as a testable hypothesis—not a replacement for laboratory validation.

Pipeline

From sequence to
engineered protein

Five steps. ESM-2 language model. Gradient-boosted scoring.

Collect known proteins

Pull real structures from RCSB PDB and AlphaFold — 200,000+ structures worldwide.

Understand with ESM-2

Meta's 650M-parameter protein language model reads each sequence and maps residue contributions to function.

Score every mutation

6,000+ amino acid swaps scored per sequence for thermal stability, catalytic activity, and PET binding affinity.

Combine and rank

Top mutations combined into multi-mutation candidates, verified by a gradient boosting ensemble at ~80% cross-validated accuracy.

Output for the lab

Ranked candidates with full protein and DNA sequences — ready for gene synthesis and experimental validation.

Target Temperature

Higher temp speeds up recycling but enzymes denature. Near the glass transition (70°C) PET chains loosen.

°C
PET glass transition — ideal for recycling
pH

Buffer pH affects charged residue stability and catalytic activity. Most PETases peak at pH 7.5–9.0 (Yoshida 2016; Tournier 2020).

pH
Slightly alkaline — optimal for most PETases
Ionic Strength (NaCl)

Salt concentration screens surface charge interactions via Debye-Hückel electrostatics. High ionic strength reduces the stabilising or destabilising effect of charged surface mutations. Industrial process water is typically 200–500 mM. (Tanford 1961; FoldX method)

mM
Physiological — standard lab buffer
Ca²⁺ Concentration (CaCl₂)

Calcium ions stabilise LCC, TfCut2, and Cut190 by coordinating conserved Asp/Glu residues at the N-terminal binding site. LCC: +14°C ΔTm at 2 mM Ca²⁺ (Kd=0.4 mM; Sulaiman 2012). TfCut2: +11°C at 5 mM (Kawai 2019). Mutations that alter chelating residues are penalised or rewarded accordingly.

mM
No Ca²⁺ — binding site correction inactive

Our Mission

PET Lab is an enzyme engineering research platform focused on the global plastic waste crisis. We design thermostable PETase variants for industrial-scale recycling — evaluating catalytic activity, thermal stability, and PET binding at the temperatures used by real recycling plants. Protein language models and gradient-boosted mutation scoring help narrow a large search space into a focused set of candidates for laboratory validation.
