🧫 Engineering heat-tough plastic-eating enzymes

A two-model AI pipeline — explained with real PET-degrading enzymes

🎯 The goal: find an enzyme that still works at ~70 °C — the temperature where PET plastic softens and breaks down fastest. Most natural PET enzymes fall apart well below that, so they're useless in a real recycling plant.

You start with candidate enzymes that can chew PET, but you don't know which can take the heat: IsPETase  ·  Thermobifida cutinase  ·  LCC.

Stage 2 · Optimize

🔧 How do we make LCC even tougher?

Mutation model — tests tiny tweaks, predicts whether each helps or hurts.

🟢 Change position 243 (F → I) +2 °C
🟢 Add a disulfide bond (238 / 283) +5 °C
🟢 Change position 210 (stabilizing) +2 °C
🔴 Change position 88 (destabilizing) −4 °C → discard
✓ Trust check: this model was validated on S669, the standard mutation benchmark.
↓
Stage 3 · Combine

➕ What's the final heat tolerance of our design?

The two models multiply in value: one gives the starting level, the other the boost.

LCC starting point (screening model) : ~75 °C + chosen mutations (mutation model) : ~+9 °C ───────────────────────────────────────────────── = engineered LCC, estimated : ~84 °C ✓ works hot

Not hypothetical: the real engineered LCC (“LCC-ICCG”) raised its melting point by ~9 °C and now runs in actual PET-recycling plants at 72 °C.

↓
Stage 4 · Confirm

✅ Rank the designs — lab-test only the winners

The models did the expensive narrowing-down (thousands of options → a few). Send only the top 1–2 candidates to the lab to confirm. AI prioritizes; the bench validates.

🔭 Screening model

“the scout”

Looks at one whole enzyme → guesses its heat tolerance. Answers: “which enzyme is worth our time?”

🛠️ Mutation model

“the engineer”

Looks at one enzyme + one change → does it help? Answers: “how do we upgrade the one we picked?”

The scout finds the toughest material → the engineer reinforces it.
Neither alone gets you there. Together, they do.

⚠️ Numbers shown are illustrative for explanation. Predicted temperatures are estimates used to rank and prioritize candidates — final winners are always confirmed experimentally.
