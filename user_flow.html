🧬 How a scientist uses the pipeline

The two-stage flow from the user's point of view — what you put in, what you get back, and what you decide at each step.

👤 You (the scientist) ⚙️ The system (models) 📋 Output / your decision
🎯 You want a plastic-degrading enzyme that still works at ~70 °C
1

🔍 Screen your candidates

Stage 1 · Screener
👤 You provide
A list of candidate enzyme sequences (e.g. a FASTA file of PETases, cutinases, LCC, or thousands of hits mined from databases). No lab data needed — just the sequences.
⚙️ System does
Reads each sequence, generates its language-model fingerprint, and predicts the melting temperature (Tm) for every one — in seconds, with no experiments.
📋 You get
A ranked table: each enzyme with its predicted heat tolerance.
LCC ~75 °C ✓ keep Thermobifida cut. ~62 °C IsPETase ~46 °C ✗ too fragile
✅ You decide
Pick the top-ranked enzyme(s) as your starting scaffold. The system is tuned for high precision, so the top of the list is trustworthy — you can confidently invest in it.
↓
🔀 Shortcut: already committed to one enzyme (e.g. you know you're using LCC)? Skip Step 1 and go straight to Step 2 — screening only matters when you're choosing among several candidates.
↓
2

🔧 Optimize the chosen enzyme

Stage 2 · Mutation
👤 You provide
The one enzyme you picked (its sequence). Optionally, point to regions to focus on (e.g. surface residues, away from the active site).
⚙️ System does
Enumerates candidate mutations (e.g. every single amino-acid change — thousands of them) and predicts each one's effect on stability (ΔΔG).
📋 You get
A ranked list of mutations, the stabilizing ones on top.
F243I +2 °C ✓ S238C/283C +5 °C ✓ (disulfide) G210P +2 °C ✓ A88V −4 °C ✗ discard
✅ You decide
Select the top stabilizing mutations and combine the best few into a candidate design.
↓
3

➕ Estimate the final design

Combine
⚙️ System does
Adds the screener's starting point to the mutation boosts:
starting Tₛ (Step 1) ~75 °C + chosen mutations ~+9 °C = engineered enzyme ~84 °C
📋 You get
An absolute predicted stability for your designed enzyme — a real number you can check against your 70 °C target.
✅ You decide
Keep the designs that clear your target (here, comfortably past 70 °C); drop the ones that don't.
↓
4

🧪 Confirm in the lab

Validate
👤 You do
Send only the top 1–2 designs to the wet lab to build and test. The models did the expensive narrowing-down; the bench just confirms the winners.
📋 Outcome
Experimental confirmation of the engineered, heat-tolerant enzyme — reached with a fraction of the lab effort.
✅ A heat-tolerant, plastic-degrading enzyme candidate — found by AI, confirmed at the bench
Why this saves you time and money: the slow, costly step is the wet lab. Both models work from sequence alone (no experiments), so you screen thousands of options and design mutations in silico, and only the few most-promising candidates ever reach the bench. The models are tuned for high precision — a short, trustworthy shortlist — so you rarely waste lab time on a dud.

Stage 1 = whole-protein screening model (sequence → Tm); Stage 2 = mutation model (sequence + mutation → ΔΔG). Predicted temperatures are estimates for ranking and prioritization; final candidates are always confirmed experimentally.
